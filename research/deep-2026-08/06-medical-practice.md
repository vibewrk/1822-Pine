THE HOUSE AS A DOCTOR'S OFFICE, AND PHYSICIANS' ROW — RESEARCH RETURN
1822 Pine Street (756 Pine before 1858), Philadelphia. All sources below were opened by me; every quote is verbatim from the source named.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
HEADLINE: 1822 PINE WAS A DOCTOR'S HOUSE FOR AT LEAST 19 CONTINUOUS YEARS (1919–1937+), AND NEITHER OF THE TWO NAMES THE REPO CARRIES IS CORRECTLY DATED
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This is a direct fill for GAP 1 (1922–1941, "the largest and most important" hole) and for the tail of GAP 3. Two Penn-trained surgeons held the address in succession, and the handover is datable to a single year.

---- FINDING 1 [A] — Dr. Henry Paul Brown, Jr. at 1822 Pine, 1919–1921. Visually verified on the page image.
American Medical Directory, 7th ed. (AMA, 1921), Philadelphia listing. I cropped and read the scanned page itself:
"BROWN, HENRY PAUL, JR., b'88; Pa.1,'12; l'12; ⊕ S★ D4—1822 Pine St.; until 10"
https://archive.org/details/americanmedicald1921ameruoft/page/n1399/mode/1up
Decoded from the directory's own key pages (also read): names in capitals = member of County and State medical societies; ⊕ = member of the American Medical Association; b'88 = born 1888; Pa.1 = "Univ. of Pa. Sch. of Med., Philadelphia", class of 1912; l'12 = licensed 1912; "S" = Surgeon ("Abbreviations signify special practice: S.—Surgeon"); D4 = "Philadelphia Pediatric Society" (from the volume's "MEMBERS OF SPECIAL SOCIETIES / D—Pediatrics and Internal Medicine" key). "until 10" is his stated consulting hour — mornings, at that address, with no separate office given.

Corroborated independently four times in the College of Physicians of Philadelphia's own Fellows roll:
- Trans. Coll. Phys. Phila., 3rd ser. vol. 41 (1919): "1919. Brown, Henry P., Jr., M.D. 1822 Pine St." https://archive.org/details/transactionsofco3411coll
- vol. 42 (1920): identical line. https://archive.org/details/transactionsofco3421coll
- vol. 43 (1921): "1919. Brown, Henry P., Jr., M.D., Assistant Surgeon, Presbyterian and Pennsylvania Hospitals; Dispensary Surgeon, Children's Hospital. 1822 Pine St." https://archive.org/details/transactionsofco3431coll
(The leading "1919." is his year of election as a Fellow.)

---- FINDING 2 [A] — Who Brown was, from Penn's own alumni register.
General Alumni Catalogue of the University of Pennsylvania, 1922:
"HENRY PAUL BROWN, Jr. b. Feb. 13, 1888; B. S., 1909; M. D., 1912; Psi Upsilon; Physician; mem. Medical Corps, U. S. A., June, 1917-Jan., 1919; mem. University Club. 1822 Pine St., Phila., Pa."
https://archive.org/details/generalalumnicat00univuoft
This is the single most narratively loaded line I found. He was in the U.S. Army Medical Corps until January 1919 and the College of Physicians roll for 1919 already gives his address as 1822 Pine. A demobilised Army surgeon took the house the year he came home.

Life dates [B, could not open the page directly — findagrave.com returns 403 to automated fetch; this is the search-index summary only]: "Dr Henry Paul Brown Jr. (1888-1955)", Find a Grave memorial #146350016, buried Falmouth, Maine. https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/146350016/henry-p-brown
Memoir citation [A, citation only; text not open-access]: Duncan GG, "Memoir of Henry Paul Brown, Jr., 1888-1955," Transactions & Studies of the College of Physicians of Philadelphia 23(4):165–166 (1956). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13324950/

Transactions of the College of Physicians of Philadelphia, 3rd ser. vol. 45 (1923), Fellows of the College. I read the scan:
"1915. PFEIFFER, DAMON B., A.B., M.D., Assistant Professor of Surgery, Graduate School of Medicine, University of Pennsylvania; Surgeon, Methodist and Abington Memorial Hospitals; Assistant Surgeon, Presbyterian and Lankenau Hospitals. 1822 Pine St."
https://archive.org/details/transactionsofco3451coll/page/n50/mode/1up

The same Fellows roll repeats 1822 Pine St. for Pfeiffer in every volume I could obtain: 1922 (vol. 44), 1923, 1924, 1925, 1926, 1927, 1928, 1929, 1930, 1931, 1932, 1934, 1935, 1936, 1937. Identifiers, all on archive.org: transactionsofco3441coll, 3451coll, 3461coll, 3471coll, 3481coll, 3491coll, 3501coll, 3511coll, 3521coll, 3531coll, 3541coll, 4121coll, 4319coll, 4419coll, 4445coll. The last of them, vol. 51 (1937), prints simply: "1915. Pfeiffer, Damon B., 1822 Pine St., Phila.*"
Independent confirmation from a different publisher — American Medical Directory, 11th ed. (AMA, 1929), Philadelphia listing:
"PFEIFFER, DAMON BECKETT, b'78; Md.7,'06; l'08; ⊕ Sx A5,28; Asst. Prof. Surg., Pa.G2—1822 Pine St.; 2-3"
https://archive.org/details/bwb_T3-BRL-424 (full text: https://archive.org/stream/bwb_T3-BRL-424/bwb_T3-BRL-424_djvu.txt)
Decoded from the same volume's key tables, which I read: Md.7 = "Johns Hopkins University Med. Dept.", class of 1906; Pa.G2 = the Penn Graduate School of Medicine; A5 = "American Surgical Association"; A28 = "American College of Surgeons". "2-3" is his stated afternoon consulting hour at that address, with no separate office address listed.
Memoir citation [A, citation only]: Deaver JM, "Memoir of Damon Beckett Pfeiffer. 1878-1966," Trans. Stud. Coll. Phys. Phila. 35:200 (1968). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4914878/

---- FINDING 4 [A] — The handover is datable to one year, and it was surgeon-to-surgeon at the same hospital.
Same source series, consecutive volumes:
- vol. 43 (1921): Brown "1822 Pine St."; Pfeiffer "…Assistant Surgeon, Presbyterian Hospital. 2028 Pine St."
- vol. 44 (1922): Brown "…Dispensary Surgeon, Children's Hospital. 2134 Spruce St."; Pfeiffer "…Assistant Surgeon, Presbyterian Hospital. 1822 Pine St."
Both men are listed as Assistant Surgeon at Presbyterian Hospital in the very volume where the address changes hands. That the practice, not just the house, passed between them is a reasonable reading but is [C] — the sources show only the two addresses swapping in the same year.
For completeness, Brown's later life: American Medical Directory 1929, "BROWN, HENRY PAUL, JR., b'88; Pa.1,'12; l'12; ⊕ Sx A28—Chestnut Hill; office, 1601 Walnut St.; 2-3" [A].

---- FINDING 5 [A/B] — What can actually be documented about the "doctor's office at this address."
What is documented [A]: for both men, 1822 Pine is given as the *primary* address with stated consulting hours and **no separate office address**. The directories' own convention distinguishes the two — e.g. in the same 1929 Philadelphia column, "Clay, Jos. Valentine Francis … 5211 Drexel Rd.; office, 1806 Pine St." and "Brown, Henry Paul, Jr. … Chestnut Hill; office, 1601 Walnut St." So the format itself establishes that 1822 Pine was a combined residence-and-consulting address: Brown saw patients there "until 10" in the morning (1921), Pfeiffer from "2-3" in the afternoon (1929).
What is NOT documented [C — do not publish as fact]: which floor. No source I reached says "parlor floor," "front room," or describes any office fit-out, and I found no building permit, alteration notice, or plan for a medical conversion at 1822 Pine. The phrase "parlor-floor doctor's office" is a period-plausible inference from Philadelphia rowhouse practice, not a sourced fact about this house. It should be written as inference or dropped.

---- FINDING 6 [A] — CORRECTION: the repo's two physician claims are both mis-dated, and one is wrong outright.
(a) "Dr. Damon B. Pfeiffer — 1822 Pine St, College of Physicians roll, by 1915." WRONG DATE, RIGHT MAN. The College of Physicians roll for 1915, 1916, 1917, 1918 and 1921 all give Pfeiffer's address as **2028 Pine St.**, e.g. Trans. vol. 37 (1915): "1915. Pfeiffer, Damon B., A.B., M.D., Instructor in Surgery, University of Pennsylvania; Assistant Surgeon, University Hospital; Pathologist to the German Hospital; Director of the Clinical Laboratory, Presbyterian Hospital. 2028 Pine St." (https://archive.org/details/transactionsofco3371coll). The American Medical Directory 1912 likewise puts him at "1953 Locust St." and the 1921 edition at "2028 Pine St." The "1915" in the repo is his year of *election as a Fellow*, printed at the head of every one of his entries — almost certainly what was misread as a date of residence. He is at 1822 Pine from 1922, not 1915.
(b) "Stillwell Corson Burns — Surgery, 1822 Pine St, 1916 register." NOT SUPPORTED, AND CONTRADICTED. The College of Physicians roll gives him 1326 Spring Garden St. in the 1914, 1915, 1916, 1917 and 1918 volumes — e.g. Trans. vol. 38 (1916): "1906. Burns, Stillwell C, M.D., Lecturer on Surgery in the Medico-Chirurgical College; Assistant Surgeon to the Medico-Chirurgical Hospital. 1326 Spring Garden St." (https://archive.org/details/transactionsofco3381coll) — and 1925 Spring Garden St. in every volume from 1919 through 1932. The American Medical Directory 1921 agrees: "Burns, Stillwell Corson (28) 1925 Spring Garden St." He never appears at 1822 Pine in any source I searched. His real credentials, for whatever use: elected Fellow 1906; Pa.11 = "Medico-Chirurgical Coll. of Philadelphia," class of 1898; by the 1918 volume "Surgeon to the Graduate School of Medicine, Polyclinic Section, University of Pennsylvania"; b. 1875 per AMD 1921.

PHYSICIANS' ROW: THE 1800 BLOCK OF PINE, MEASURED
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
---- FINDING 7 [A, with an OCR caveat] — A countable growth curve from one consistent source series.
I extracted every Philadelphia entry naming an address on the 1800 block of Pine from the AMA's American Medical Directory, Philadelphia alphabetical listing, three editions:
- 3rd ed., 1912: 10 physician entries across 8 house numbers (1801, 1804×2, 1806, 1820, 1824×2, 1825, 1832, 1836). 1822 Pine: none.
- 7th ed., 1921: 15 entries across 13 house numbers (1801, 1802, 1803, 1804×2, 1806, 1809, 1818, 1820, **1822**, 1823, 1824×2, 1825, 1828).
- 11th ed., 1929: 49 entries across 23 house numbers, 1801 to 1830. Densest: 1826 Pine (7 physicians) and 1829 Pine (7) — by 1929 both are functioning as multi-doctor office buildings, with most of their occupants listing a home elsewhere and 1826/1829 Pine as "office." 1822 Pine: 1 (Pfeiffer), and it is a *residence-office*, not a suite.
Caveat to publish with the numbers: these are counts from OCR'd text, hand-checked at the margins, so treat them as "at least N," not exact. Sources: https://archive.org/details/americanmedicald1912ameruoft , https://archive.org/details/americanmedicald1921ameruoft , https://archive.org/details/bwb_T3-BRL-424
The story the numbers tell: 1822 Pine did not lead this transformation, it joined it. The block was already medical when the Davis household was still there, and the house converted at the exact moment the block was going from a scatter of doctors to a district.

---- FINDING 8 [A] — Direct next-door neighbours, named, 1912–1937. The row is genuinely a row.
From the same two source series, all verbatim:
- 1824 Pine (immediate east neighbour, and a doctor's house across the whole period): "JOPSON, JOHN H., b'71; Pa.1,'93; l'93; ⊕ S★ A5,28,30; Prof. Clin. Surg., Pa.1 and Prof. Surg., Pa.G2—1824 Pine St" (AMD 1929). Jopson is also listed at 1824 Pine in AMD 1912 and 1921, and in the College of Physicians roll with "Children's Hospitals. 1824 Pine St." He is the Professor of Clinical Surgery at Penn, living two doors from 1822.
- 1820 Pine (immediate west neighbour — the same house that served as Alice Paul's Congressional Union Pennsylvania headquarters in 1914–15): a continuous line of physicians. AMD 1912: "Lewis, Robert Morton (b'86)—Pa.1,'10; (l'10); 1820 Pine St.; office, University Hospital." Trans. vol. 37 (1915): "1904. Cruice, John M., M.D., Physician to the Henry Phipps Institute of the University of Pennsylvania… 1820 Pine St." Trans. vols. 48–53 (1926–1931): William Drayton, Jr., M.D. at 1820 Pine. Trans. vol. 51 (1937): "1936. White, Ellen Pawling Corson-, 1820 Pine St., Phila."
- 1825 Pine: "TAYLOR, WM. J., b'61; Pa.1,'82; l'82; S★ A3,5,17, D4—1825 Pine St.; 9-11" (AMD 1921), still there in 1929 and 1935.
- 1804 Pine: "NEWLIN, ARTHUR, b'74; Pa.1,'99; l'99; ⊕ D4, F10—1804 Pine St.; 2-3" (AMD 1921) — at the same address in 1912, 1929 and 1937, a 25-year run.
- 1801 Pine: "MUSSON, EMMA E. (b'61)—Pa.7,'83; (l'83); 1801 Pine St." (AMD 1912) — a Woman's Medical College graduate on the block in 1912.

---- FINDING 9 [A for the listing; B for the identification] — Dr. Martha Tracy, Dean of the Woman's Medical College of Pennsylvania, at 1820 Pine Street in 1921.
American Medical Directory, 7th ed. (1921), Philadelphia listing:
"TRACY, MARTHA, b'76; Pa.7,'04; ⊕ F2; Prof. Phys., Chem. and Hyg., Pa.7—1820 Pine St; office, N. College Ave. and 21st St"
https://archive.org/details/americanmedicald1921ameruoft
Pa.7 = "Woman's Med. Coll. of Pa., Philadelphia" (from the volume's college key, which I read). The identification is near-certain but is [B] because it rests on this one listing: Wikipedia gives "Martha Tracy (April 10, 1876 – March 22, 1942)", Bryn Mawr 1894–98, "graduated in 1904 as a medical doctor" from WMCP, and "In 1917, Martha Tracy was selected as the seventh dean of the Woman's Medical College of Pennsylvania… until 1940" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Tracy ; see also https://drexel.edu/legacy-center/the-collections/exhibits/martha-tracy-medical-student-to-dean/ and https://www.amwa-doc.org/faces/martha-tracy-md-drph/). Born 1876, WMCP 1904, professor at WMCP — every datum in the directory entry matches the Dean, and no second Martha Tracy appears in the volume. By the 1929 edition she had moved: "Dean and Prof. Prev. Med., Pa.7—Hannah Penn House; office, 2101 N. College."
A second woman physician is listed at 1820 Pine in the same 1921 volume, in the Pennsylvania state-at-large section: "POTTER, ELLEN CULVER, b'[7]1; Pa.7,'03; l'04; ⊕—(1820 Pine St., Philadelphia); office, Dept. of Health." [A for the line; the identification with Ellen C. Potter, later Pennsylvania Secretary of Welfare, is [B] and I did not verify it further.]
This is a significant neighbour finding for the site's suffrage material and it is *sound* in a way the suffrage-in-the-parlour claims are not: two women physicians from the Woman's Medical College sharing the house next door, in the same house that had been the Congressional Union's Pennsylvania headquarters six years earlier.

---- FINDING 10 [B] — The medical-geography frame, from a citable free secondary source.
Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia, "City of Medicine," by Steven J. Peitzman — https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/themes/city-of-medicine/
"Most row-house Philadelphians went to family doctors in their neighborhood (or the doctor came to them)."
"But it was the downtown physicians and surgeons, most with senior faculty positions at Penn or Jefferson, who built Philadelphia's reputation as the nation's medical capital."
That second sentence is exactly the class of man who occupied 1822 Pine: Brown and Pfeiffer were both Penn faculty or Penn-hospital staff living downtown.

---- FINDING 11 [A] — Bonus, bearing on the repo's open question #8 ("Who is G. C. Purviss?"): he was not a physician.
I searched the American Medical Directory for 1912, 1921 and 1929 for every Purvis/Purviss in Pennsylvania. There is no Philadelphia physician of that name in any of the three. Whatever G. C. Purviss was to 1822 Pine in 1905, he was not a doctor practising there.
