The decisive move was not web search. It was downloading the OCR full text of thirteen Philadelphia city directories from the Internet Archive and grepping them by street number:

McElroy's Philadelphia City Directory, 1853, 1854, 1855, 1856, 1857, 1858, 1859, 1860, 1862, 1864, 1865, 1867 — https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1855amce (change the year in the identifier: mcelroysphiladelYYYYamce)
Gopsill's Philadelphia City Directory for 1871 — https://archive.org/details/gopsillsphiladel00cost

All are free, public-domain, page-image-linkable, and republishable. Every quote below is verbatim OCR from those files (OCR errors preserved and marked). Leaf numbers were resolved via IA's search-inside API; the deep links carry a `?q=` so they self-verify when opened.

This matters for grading: a city-directory line that carries its own occupational identifier ("melter & refiner, U. S. Mint"; "prof. Penn. University"; "maj. gen. u.s.a."; "(Drexel & Co.)") is not "a name in a directory." It is the identification, printed by the source. Those I grade A. Where the directory gives only a name, I grade lower and say so.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 1 — THE FIVE CANDIDATE NEIGHBORS: VERDICTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

Odd numbers are the north side of Pine, even the south. This follows from the 1985 deed already in the archive ("south side of Pine, 230 feet west of Eighteenth" for 1822). Grade B inference, but a safe one. So Booth, Foulke, Drexel and Biddle were all ACROSS the street from 1822 Pine; Coppée was on the same side, two doors west.

───────────────────────────────
1. JAMES CURTIS BOOTH, 1813 PINE — CONFIRMED. [A]
───────────────────────────────
Years of residence: appears in the volumes for 1859, 1860, 1862, 1864. Absent 1858 and earlier; gone by 1865.

The clinching line, because the directory supplies the identification itself:

"Booth  James  C,  melter  &  refiner,  U.  S.  Mint,  h  1813  Pine"
— McElroy's 1860, leaf 120. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1860amce/page/n120/mode/1up?q=%22melter+%26+refiner%22

Supporting run:
"Booth  James  C,  laboratory,  office  10  Chant,  h  1813  Pine" — McElroy's 1859
"Booth  James  C,  U.  S.  Mint,  h  1813  Pine" — McElroy's 1862
"Booth  James  C,  chenyst,  10  C^iant,  h  1813  Pine" — McElroy's 1864 ("chenyst" = chemist)

Corroboration that this is the right James Curtis Booth: "In 1849, he was appointed melter and refiner in the Mint by President Zachary Taylor, working there until he retired in 1887." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Curtis_Booth [B]. He was the only holder of that office in those years.

Arrival and departure both check out. In the 1858 volume he has only a laboratory listing, "Booth James C, 10th rear St. Stephen's Church," and no Pine Street house — so he moved onto the block c. 1858–59. By the 1865 volume 1813 Pine is occupied by "Williams Annie, gentlewoman" and "Williams Ellen P."; Booth later died at his home in West Haverford in 1888.

Pre-renumbering check: 1813 − 1066 = 747 Pine. There is no 747 Pine entry in the 1855, 1856 or 1857 volumes. Consistent with a c.1859 arrival, not an earlier one.

───────────────────────────────
2. WILLIAM PARKER FOULKE, 1827 PINE — CONFIRMED, AND HE WAS LIVING THERE IN 1858, THE YEAR OF THE HADROSAURUS DIG. [A]
───────────────────────────────
Years: 1858 through his death 18 June 1865; his widow held the house at least through 1871.

"FOULKE  WM.  PARKER,  1827  Pine"
— McElroy's 1858, leaf 248. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1858amce/page/n248/mode/1up?q=%22FOULKE+WM.+PARKER%22
(Printed in capitals, i.e. a paid/emphasised listing.)

"Foulke  Wm.  Parker,  1827  Pine" — McElroy's 1859
"Foulke  William  Parker,  att'y-at-law,  1827  Pine" — McElroy's 1862
"Fouike  William  Parker,  gentleman,  1827  Pine" — McElroy's 1865
"Foulke  Wm.  Parker,  Mrs.,  1827  Pine" — McElroy's 1867 (i.e. the widow)
"Foulde Julia, wid William P., h 1827 Pine" — Gopsill's 1871, leaf 577. https://archive.org/details/gopsillsphiladel00cost/page/n577/mode/1up?q=%22wid+William+P.%22

The identification is nailed by three independent name matches at the one address. Find a Grave memorial #48622517 gives:
— "William Parker Foulke Birth 31 May 1816 … Death 18 Jun 1865 … Burial Laurel Hill East Cemetery … Plot Section 7, Lot 223 & 224"
— Spouse: "Julia De Veaux Powel Foulke 1833 – 1884"  → matches "Foulde Julia, wid William P., h 1827 Pine," 1871
— Sibling: "Francis Edward Foulke 1834 – 1893"  → matches "Foulke Francis E., 1827 Pine," McElroy's 1858 and 1860
— Bio: "He discovered the first full dinosaur skeleton in North America (Hadrosaurus foulkii, which means 'Foulke's big lizard') in Haddonfield, New Jersey in 1858."
https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/48622517/william-parker-foulke [B for the memorial; A for the directory address]

So: the man who dug up the first substantially complete dinosaur skeleton in North America was, in the year he did it, living directly across Pine Street from 1822. This is the single strongest neighbor finding.

Pre-renumbering check: 1827 − 1066 = 761 Pine. No 761 Pine entry appears in 1855–1857. He arrived on the block c. 1857–58 — the year before the dig.

───────────────────────────────
3. HENRY COPPÉE, 1826 PINE — CONFIRMED, AND HE WAS TWO DOORS FROM 1822. [A]
───────────────────────────────
Years: 1857 through 1864. This is the ONE candidate on the same side of the street as 1822 Pine.

Pre-renumbering:
"Coppee  Henry,  prof.  Penn.  University,  760  Pine"
— McElroy's 1857, leaf 161. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1857amce/page/n161/mode/1up?q=%22760+Pine%22

Post-renumbering, same man, same house:
"Coppee  Hen.,  prof.  Penn.  University,  1826  Pmo" — McElroy's 1858 ("Pmo" is OCR for "Pine")
"Coppee  Hen.,  prof.  Penn.  University,  1826  Pine" — McElroy's 1859
"Coppee Henry, professor Penn University, h … 1826 Pine" — McElroy's 1860
"Coppee  Henry,  professor  Univ.  Penna.,  1826  Pine" — McElroy's 1862
"Coppee  Henry,  professer  Univer.  Pa.,  1826  Pine" — McElroy's 1864

The directory names his position, and it matches: "After resigning from the U.S. Army, he was the professor of English literature and history at the University of Pennsylvania in Philadelphia, from 1855 to 1866. … In 1866, Coppée was selected by Asa Packer to serve as the first president of Lehigh University." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Copp%C3%A9e [B]

Departure confirmed: by the 1865 volume he is at "Coppee ILnry, professor, Univer Pa., S 9th," and 1826 Pine is occupied by "Conyngham A. H. Miss." He left the block a year or two before leaving Philadelphia for Bethlehem.

Note the neat arithmetic: John Roset at 756 Pine and Henry Coppée at 760 Pine in 1857 → 1822 Pine and 1826 Pine in 1858, with Jonathan Barnes at 758/1824 between them. Two doors apart, for seven years.

───────────────────────────────
4. CHAPMAN BIDDLE, 1831 PINE — CONFIRMED. [A for address; B for the Gettysburg identification, but tight]
───────────────────────────────
Years: continuously 1858 through 1871 at least; his widow died there in 1894.

"BIDDLE  CHAPMAN,  atty.  &  coun.  131  S  5th,  h  1831  Pine"
— McElroy's 1858, leaf 72. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1858amce/page/n72/mode/1up?q=%22BIDDLE+CHAPMAN%22
Same line recurs in 1859, 1860, 1862, 1864, 1865, 1867, and:
"Biddle Chapman, lawyer, 131 S 5th, h 1831 Pine" — Gopsill's 1871

The directory gives only "attorney & counsellor," so the tie to the colonel needs an outside source. Two, independently:
— "Chapman Biddle (January 22, 1822 – December 9, 1880) … He studied law, passed his bar exam in 1848, and established a private practice before the outbreak of the war. … He commanded a brigade of infantry at the Battle of Gettysburg. … burial_place = Church of St. James the Less, Philadelphia" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman_Biddle [B]
— Find a Grave #102878937, transcribing a death/burial record: "Mary Livingston Cochran Biddle, age seventy four (9), widow, died April 4th 1894. Address: 1831 Pine St, Philadelphia 7th Ward. Burial: April 7, 1894 at St. James the Less Cemetery." Her linked spouse is "Chapman Biddle 1822 – 1880 ( m. 1849)". https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/102878937/mary-livingston-biddle [B]

A Philadelphia lawyer named Chapman Biddle, admitted 1848, at 1831 Pine, whose widow dies at 1831 Pine and is buried in the same churchyard as the colonel — that is the same man. I'd publish it, with the sourcing shown.

CAUTION: his death date is given as December 9, 1880 by Wikipedia's infobox and December 29, 1880 by other summaries. Do not publish a day without checking. He was in the field during 1862–63 while the listing persisted, so the directory documents the household, not his physical presence in the war years.

───────────────────────────────
5. JOSEPH W. DREXEL, 1829 PINE — CONFIRMED, BUT THE DATES ARE LATER THAN THE ARCHIVE ASSUMES. [A]
───────────────────────────────
He was NOT there in the Roset-family era of the 1850s and 1860s. He arrives c. 1866–67.

"D.exel  Joseph  W.,  broker,  34  S  3d,  h  1829  Pine" — McElroy's 1867 (OCR damage on "Drexel"; the adjacent lines read "Dre.xel Frank A., banker, 34 S 3d. h 1503 AValnut" and "Dre.xel S. M., Mrs., 1900 S Rittenhouse sq," so the run is unambiguous)

"Drexel Joseph W. (Drexel & Co.), h 1829 Pine"
— Gopsill's 1871, leaf 495. https://archive.org/details/gopsillsphiladel00cost/page/n495/mode/1up?q=%22Drexel+Joseph+W%22

The 1871 line carries the firm name, which is the identification. Corroborated: "Joseph William Drexel (January 24, 1833 – March 25, 1888) was a banker, philanthropist, and book collector. … Joseph Drexel was a partner in the firm of Drexel, Morgan and Company, where his brother, Anthony, was senior partner. In 1876 … Joseph retired from the business" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_William_Drexel [B]

Who was at 1829 Pine BEFORE him: "Quin Joseph P., 248 Chestnut, h 1829 Pine" (1858); "Ashmead Thomas E., merchant, from 1432 Chestnut to 1829 Pine" (1860); Ashmead through 1865. So the Drexel occupancy of 1829 Pine begins c. 1866.

THE STORY THIS UNLOCKS: Anthony J. Drexel married Ellen Rozet, daughter of John Roset of 1822 Pine. From c. 1867 Anthony's younger brother Joseph was living directly across the street from his sister-in-law's father's house. That is a documented, non-fabricated Drexel-to-1822-Pine adjacency — and it is available WITHOUT the forbidden claim that any Drexel lived at 1822 Pine.

PART 2 — EXTENSIONS: OTHER VERIFIED NOTABLES ON THESE BLOCKS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

I kept this short on purpose. These are the ones where the directory carries its own identification or an outside source closes the loop. Several are more consequential than the five I was asked to check.

── MAJ. GEN. GEORGE GORDON MEADE, 1836 DELANCEY PLACE, 1864/65–1872 [A] ──
The commanding general at Gettysburg lived one block north of 1822 Pine, on the 1800 block of Delancey, for the last eight years of his life.

"MEADE  GEORGE  G.,  Major-Gen.  U.  S.  A.,  1836  De  Lancey  pi"
— McElroy's 1865, leaf 505. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1865amce/page/n505/mode/1up?q=%22MEADE+GEORGE+G%22
"Meade  George  G.,  Major-General,  1103  Girard,  h  1836  De  Lancey  pi" — McElroy's 1867
"Meade George G., maj. gen. u.s.a., S Broad n Walnut, h 1836 De Lancey pl" — Gopsill's 1871, leaf 1029. https://archive.org/details/gopsillsphiladel00cost/page/n1029/mode/1up?q=%22maj.+gen%22

Absent from the 1859, 1860, 1862 and 1864 volumes — so he took the house c. 1864–65, which fits the well-known story that citizens presented it to him after the war. There is a PHMC marker (see Part 4). He died there 6 or 7 November 1872 (sources differ; do not publish a day without checking).

── CHARLES SHORT, 1808 PINE, 1858–1862 — CO-AUTHOR OF "LEWIS AND SHORT" [A] ──
Same side of the street as 1822, seven doors east. This is a genuinely new find and probably the most publishable of the extensions.

"Short  Charles,  private  classical  school,  1205  Chestnut,  h  1808  Pine"
— McElroy's 1858, leaf 645. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1858amce/page/n645/mode/1up?q=%22private+classical+school%22
"Short  Charles,  teacher,  1808  Pine" — McElroy's 1860
"Short  Charles,  Classical  School,  N  W  Chestnut  &  12th,  h  1808  Pine" — McElroy's 1862

Identification, from the Rutgers Database of Classical Scholars, entry "SHORT, Charles Lancaster," b. May 28 1821 Haverhill MA, d. December 24 1886 New York:
"headmaster Roxbury Lat. Sch., 1848-53; master of own school (Philadelphia), 1853-63; pres. & prof, mental & moral philos. Kenyon Coll., 1863-7; prof. Lat. Columbia Coll., 1867-86"
https://dbcs.rutgers.edu/all-scholars/9118-short-charles-lancaster [A — scholarly reference database]

The fit is exact on both ends: a man running his own school in Philadelphia 1853–63, listed at 1808 Pine with a private classical school 1858–62, and gone from 1808 Pine in the 1864 volume — the year after he left for Kenyon. He is the "Short" of A Latin Dictionary (1879), still the standard Latin–English lexicon.

── WILLIAM DUANE, 1800 PINE (corner of 18th), 1854–1867 at least [A address / B identification] ──
Fourteen years at the corner house of the block, same side as 1822.

"Duane  William,  att'y  at  law,  138  Walnut,  h  734  Pine  ab  18th" — McElroy's 1854 and 1855 (note "ab 18th" — the directory itself locates the old 700 block at 18th Street)
"DUANE  WILLIAM,  att'y  at  law,  514  Walnut  h  1800  Pine"
— McElroy's 1858, leaf 204. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1858amce/page/n204/mode/1up?q=%22DUANE+WILLIAM%22
Recurs 1859, 1860, 1862, 1864, 1865, 1867. Also "Duane  Charles  W.,  Rev.,  1800  Pine" (1864, 1865).

Identification: the American Philosophical Society's Duane Family Papers describes the family line as "William Duane, his son William John Duane, grandson William Duane (1808-1882), and great-grandson Charles Williams Duane" — https://as.amphilsoc.org/repositories/2/resources/3232 and https://as.amphilsoc.org/agents/people/2893 [B]. Both "William Duane" the lawyer/antiquarian and "Rev. Charles W. Duane" appear at 1800 Pine, which matches that father-and-son pair. This is the grandson of William Duane of the Aurora and the son of William J. Duane, Andrew Jackson's Treasury Secretary who refused to remove the deposits. I'd label it "very probably" rather than flat assertion until a document names him at the address.

── ARCHIBALD McELROY, 1806 PINE — THE DIRECTORY PUBLISHER HIMSELF [A] ──
The archive's existing lead is confirmed exactly, and it doubles as proof of the renumbering.

"M'ELROY  THOMAS  E.,  740  Pine  W  18th" and "THE PHILADELPHIA CITY DIRECTORY, OFFICE, 7 HART'S BUILDINGS, (2d story), h 740 Pine W 18th" — McElroy's 1854
"M-ELROY  ARCHIBALD,  Publisher  of  the  Philadelphia City Directory,  office  432  Chestnut  2d  story,  Ii  1806  Pine"
— McElroy's 1858, leaf 454. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1858amce/page/n454/mode/1up?q=%22Publisher+of+the%22
740 + 1066 = 1806. The publisher of the directory renumbered his own house in his own book.

── COMMODORE DAVID GEISINGER, USN, 742 PINE (= 1808 PINE), 1855–1857 [A] ──
The archive's existing lead, confirmed, with the directory supplying the rank:
"Geisinger  D.,  commodore  U.  S.  Navy,  h  742  Pine"
— McElroy's 1857, leaf 272. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1857amce/page/n272/mode/1up?q=%22commodore+U.+S.+Navy%22
Also 1855 (with "Geisinger Geo. F., 742 Pine") and 1856. Note 742 + 1066 = 1808 — the SAME HOUSE Charles Short occupied from 1858. Two notables, one address, consecutive.

── S. AUSTIN ALLIBONE, 1816 SPRUCE, 1865–1867 [A address / B identification] ──
"Allibone  S.  Austin,  gentleman,  1816  Spruce"
— McElroy's 1865, leaf 75. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1865amce/page/n75/mode/1up?q=%22Allibone+S.+Austin%22
Also "Allibone Austin, gentleman, 1816 Spruce" — McElroy's 1867. Samuel Austin Allibone compiled A Critical Dictionary of English Literature and British and American Authors and later ran the Lenox Library in New York. The name is distinctive enough that I am fairly confident, but I did not find an independent source naming the address — mark as probable.

── JOHN S. HART, LL.D., 1828 PINE, 1858–1865 [A] ──
Directly next door to Coppée, four doors from 1822.
"Hart  John  S.,  principal  of  the  High  School,  Broad  &  Green,  h  1828  Pine" — McElroy's 1858
"HART  JOHN  S.,  Sunday School Times,  148  S  4th,  h  1828  Pine" — McElroy's 1862
And in the same 1858 volume's faculty roster: "John S. Hart, LL.D., Principal and Professor of Moral & Political Science, 1828 Pine." Principal of Philadelphia's Central High School, later editor of the Sunday School Times. Self-identifying line. Solid, if second-tier notability.

── FREDERICK A. PACKARD, 1804 PINE, 1854–1865 [A] ──
"Packard  Frederick  A.,  Am.  S.  S.  U.,  316  Chestnut,  h  738  Pine" — McElroy's 1855 (738 + 1066 = 1804)
"Packard  Frederick  A.,  cor.  sec.  &  editor  A.S.S.U.,  1122  Chestnut,  h  1804  Pine" — McElroy's 1864
Corresponding secretary and editor of the American Sunday School Union — a nationally significant publishing office in the period. Eleven years on the block.

── ONE I EXPLICITLY REFUTE: DAVID BELL BIRNEY AT 1810 DELANCEY ──
Gopsill's 1871 reads "Birney D. B., wid David B., h 1810 Delancey pl," which invites the claim that Maj. Gen. David Bell Birney lived at 1810 Delancey. He did not. The directories place the household elsewhere throughout his life: "Birney  David  B.,  com.  of  deeds,  37½ S 3d,  h  1920 [Race]" (1862); "Birney  D.  B.,  U  S  Army,  1920  Race" (1864); "Birney  D.  B.  Mrs.,  1920  Race" (1865). He died in October 1864. His WIDOW moved to 1810 Delancey afterwards. Do not publish "General Birney lived on the 1800 block of Delancey."

PART 3 — THE ROSENBACH BROTHERS: THE COMMON DATE IS WRONG
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
[B — good secondary sources, no primary in hand]

The brothers did not live at 2008–2010 Delancey from the start, and 2008 was not theirs at all.

"Dr. Abraham Simon Wolf Rosenbach and his older brother Philip first became residents of the block in 1926 when they purchased 2006 Delancey Place."
"After living there for roughly 20 years, the brothers moved a few doors down into a bigger, grander home at 2010 Delancey Place in 1949."
"It is inside this house where the Rosenbach Museum & Library was established in 1954."
— Hidden City Philadelphia, "With Bloomsday Near, Remembering The Rosenbach Brothers," June 2017. https://hiddencityphila.org/2017/06/with-bloomsday-near-remembering-the-rosenbach-brothers/

The institution's own site:
"The Rosenbach brothers' 1865 townhouse at 2010 Delancey Place is listed on the National Register of Historic Places"
"The Rosenbach was founded in 1954 by Dr. A.S.W. Rosenbach (1876-1952) and his brother, Philip (1863-1953)."
"Since 2003, the adjacent building at 2008 Delancey Place offers spaces for public programs and exhibitions."
— https://www.rosenbach.org/about/

UNRESOLVED CONFLICT: a search summary of the Hidden City piece said the foundation purchased 2008 Delancey in January 1993, while rosenbach.org says "since 2003." I could not reconcile these; do not publish either date without checking.

CORRECTED PHRASING FOR THE SITE: "Philip and A.S.W. Rosenbach lived at 2006 Delancey Place from 1926, moved to 2010 Delancey Place in 1949, and the museum opened in that house in 1954; 2008 Delancey was added later." Also note this is the 2000 block of Delancey — two blocks west of 1822 Pine, not the 1800 block.

PART 5 — FOUR THINGS I FOUND THAT ARE OUTSIDE MY LENS BUT TOO IMPORTANT TO OMIT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

These bear directly on Open Questions 1, 2, 3 and Gap 5 in the archive map. All are Grade A, free, and linkable.

── 5a. THE RENUMBERING IS NOW PROVEN FROM A SINGLE PAIR OF PAGES, WITH JOHN ROSET AS THE PROOF ──

"Roset  John,  mer.,  756  Pine"
— McElroy's 1855, leaf 495. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1855amce/page/n495/mode/1up?q=%22756+Pine%22
(Also in the 1856 and 1857 volumes: "Roset John, mer^ 756 Pine" / "Roset  John,  mer.,  756  Pine")

"Roset  John,  nier.,  1822  Pine"
— McElroy's 1858, leaf 612. https://archive.org/details/mcelroysphiladel1858amce/page/n612/mode/1up?q=%22Roset+John%22
("nier." is OCR for "mer.")

Same man, same trade, consecutive volumes, +1066. And the whole block converts in lockstep between the 1857 and 1858 volumes:
734 Duane → 1800 · 736 Hockley → 1802 · 738 Packard → 1804 · 740 McElroy → 1806 · 742 Geisinger → 1808 · 744 Siter → 1810 · 746 Fling → 1812 · 748 Rev. Jenkins → 1814 · 750 Kester → 1816 · 754 Hone → 1820 · 756 ROSET → 1822 · 758 Barnes → 1824 · 760 Coppée → 1826 · 766 Robeno → 1832 · 768 Taylor → 1834 · 770 Hazlehurst → 1836.
Sixteen households, one offset, two consecutive volumes. That is a publishable table, and it is better evidence than the eight-household table currently in the repo.

This also independently confirms JOHN ROSET, MERCHANT, AT THE HOUSE FROM 1855 — a fact the site currently rests entirely on an upside-down, unread deed card.

── 5b. THE BUILD DATE: THE 1853 VOLUME HAS NOTHING ON THE BLOCK. THE 1854 VOLUME DOES. [A for the data; B for the inference] ──
Highest Pine Street numbers appearing in the 1853 volume's name section: 702, 704, 706, 707, 708 — then a gap to 818. Nothing in the 730–779 range at all.
1854 volume: 734, 736, 738, 740, 746, 748, 750, 752, 760 appear — the row is filling in from the 18th Street corner.
1855 volume: 736, 738, 740, 742, 744, 746, 748, 750, 754, 756 (Roset), 758, 762, 766, 768, 770 — the block is essentially full.
1822 Pine (as 756) is first occupied in the volume for 1855. This is consistent with an April 1854 deed and inconsistent with "c. 1845." Caveat: this is negative OCR evidence over a 3 MB text; a spot-check of the 1853 volume's actual Pine Street pages would harden it.

── 5c. HOWARD SPENCER IS DOCUMENTED AT 1822 PINE IN 1871 — THE NAMING PROBLEM IS RESOLVED [A] ──

"Spencer Howard, pres, 233 S 3d, h 1822 Pine"
— Gopsill's Philadelphia City Directory for 1871, leaf 1354. https://archive.org/details/gopsillsphiladel00cost/page/n1354/mode/1up?q=%22Spencer+Howard%22

And four years earlier, same man, same office, different house:
"Spencer  How.ird,  pres.  A.  K.  Co.,  233  S  3d,  h  Burlington,  X  J" — McElroy's 1867 ("X J" = N J)

So Howard Spencer, a company president with an office at 233 South Third Street, moved his household from Burlington, New Jersey, into 1822 Pine between 1867 and 1871. CONTENT-OPPORTUNITIES #17 is right and DEEP-RESEARCH-REPORT's "rename Howard to Graham throughout" instruction is wrong: both men are real, and Howard is the earlier one. Graham Spencer's 1893 purchase is a separate, later event.

── 5d. 1822 PINE WAS A TENANCY SUCCESSION, NOT A ROSET FAMILY HOME, FROM 1859 [A] ──

John Roset appears at the house for four volumes only (1855–1858) and is gone from it by 1859, eleven years before his death. PART 6 — ARTICLE IDEAS THIS LENS SUPPORTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

1. "The Dinosaur Man Across the Street." William Parker Foulke was living at 1827 Pine — across from 1822 — in 1858, the year he excavated Hadrosaurus foulkii, the first substantially complete dinosaur skeleton found in North America. Fully sourced from the 1858 directory plus Find a Grave (wife Julia, brother Francis E., both at the same address). Pairs with the Edward Drinker Cope marker two blocks west at 2100 Pine. This is the strongest single story on the block and nothing on the site currently uses it.

2. "Who Lived on This Block in 1858: A Complete Reconstruction." A house-by-house table of the 1800 block of Pine from the 1858 McElroy's, with the +1066 conversion to the pre-1858 numbers beside it. Nineteen households, sixteen of them convertible, every line quotable verbatim and every page linkable to the Internet Archive. Doubles as the definitive public proof of the renumbering — the site's single biggest unexploited research key — and as the source page every future article cites.

3. "Two Doors Down: Henry Coppée at 1826 Pine." Lehigh's first president, then a Penn professor of English literature, lived two doors west of 1822 Pine from 1857 to 1864, with Jonathan Barnes at 1824 in between. The directory names his job in its own line. A short, tight, unfalsifiable piece.

4. "The Man Who Wrote Half the Latin Dictionary Lived at 1808 Pine." Charles Lancaster Short, co-author of Lewis and Short, ran his classical school from 1205 Chestnut and lived seven doors east of 1822 from 1858 to 1862, in the same house Commodore David Geisinger had occupied through 1857. Two notables, one address, consecutive — and Short's dates match the Rutgers scholarly database to the year.

5. "The Gettysburg Block." Chapman Biddle at 1831 Pine, who took a head wound commanding a brigade on McPherson's Ridge, and George Gordon Meade at 1836 Delancey Place one block north — plus the Civil War Library and Museum at 1805 Pine from 1922 to 2008 and the (possibly missing) John Page Nicholson marker there. Four Civil War anchors within 600 feet, three with primary directory citations.

6. "The House Was Rented: 1822 Pine, 1859–1893." The tenancy succession — Roset, Broadhead, the McNeills, S. Decatur Smith the glass manufacturer, Mary Cuthbert, James Trimble the grocer, Howard Spencer. Corrects the site's own narrative using primary sources, which is exactly the kind of piece a no-fabrication archive should be publishing.

7. "Howard Spencer, 1871." A short correction piece resolving the site's own flagged contradiction, built on one directory line and its 1867 predecessor showing him in Burlington, New Jersey. Cheap, decisive, and it closes Open Question 1.

8. "The Drexels Across the Street." Joseph W. Drexel of Drexel & Co. at 1829 Pine from c. 1867. Lets the site write about a Drexel and this block truthfully, without ever claiming a Drexel lived at 1822.
