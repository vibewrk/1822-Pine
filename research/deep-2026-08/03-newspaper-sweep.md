── A1. GRADE A — Graham Spencer's obituary. Resolves Open Question #1 outright, and corrects the site's death date by five months.
Wilmington (Del.) Evening Journal, Tue Nov 24, 1896, p.2.
https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85042354/1896-11-24/ed-1/?sp=2
Image crop (verified by eye): https://tile.loc.gov/image-services/iiif/service:ndnp:deu:batch_deu_bear_ver01:data:sn85042354:00383343380:1896112401:0869/1900,5240,900,940/full/0/default.jpg
Verbatim: "GRAHAM SPENCER DEAD. HE DEVELOPED THE KAOLIN MINES, AT HOCKESSIN, AND MADE A FORTUNE. The funeral of Graham Spencer, who for many years was connected with the kaolin industry, at Hockessin, took place from the residence of his relatives, at No. 1822 Pine street, Philadelphia, this morning, at 11 o'clock. Mr. Spencer was 44 years old and was the son of Howard Spencer, who was the pioneer in the kaolin and spar industry in this section. His father developed the kaolin mines and derived a large fortune from the sale of the product. The son continued the business and opened several new fields in Delaware and Pennsylvania. Graham Spencer formerly made his home in Wilmington in Winter. His father [di]ed but a year ago."
What this settles:
- Howard and Graham Spencer are FATHER AND SON, not one man mis-transcribed. CONTENT-OPPORTUNITIES #17 is right and DEEP-RESEARCH-REPORT's "rename Howard → Graham throughout" instruction is wrong. Howard Spencer = the kaolin/feldspar pioneer at Hockessin, Delaware, died c. 1895. Graham Spencer = son, b. c. 1852, d. Nov 1896.
- The site's "Graham Spencer died June 28, 1896" is WRONG. The funeral was the morning of Nov 24, 1896, so he died c. Nov 21–23. This explains the archive's UNREAD Philadelphia Times clipping of **1896-11-23 p.11** (npcom 53387473) — almost certainly the death notice — and means the **1896-06-29** Times clipping is NOT his obituary. Reading 1896-11-23 is now the single highest-value unread clipping in the archive.
- The funeral was held IN the house. That is a documented event at 1822 Pine that the site does not currently narrate.
- Careful: the phrase is "the residence of his relatives," not "his residence." Quote it as printed; do not upgrade it to "his home."
- Bonus: this ties Mrs. Graham Spencer's Chester County country house at Kaolin, Pa. (the site's 1901 Times clipping) to the family's Hockessin, Del. kaolin fortune — the same clay district, five miles apart.

- This single free page replaces four separate [C]-grade assertions in Part 3 of the archive map with one [A] source.

D. CONCRETE ARTICLE IDEAS THIS LENS SUPPORTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════
Each is fully sourced above, pre-1930, and republishable with page images.

1. "Graham Spencer Dead" — the kaolin fortune that bought the house. Howard Spencer, Hockessin's clay pioneer; his son Graham, dead at 44 in November 1896; the funeral held at 1822 Pine on the morning of Nov 24. Corrects the site's June 1896 date and ends the Howard-vs-Graham confusion with one document. Lead image: the Wilmington obituary crop.

2. "Suddenly, at 1822 Pine Street" — May 21, 1918. The death of Naomi Lawton Davis, told from the two death notices, with the May 1917 chambermaid ad as the household's last ordinary trace. Fills the archive's largest narrative hole with two documents instead of none.

3. "The House Next Door: Dora Lewis of 1820 Pine." The woman who funded the 1915 Philadelphia canvass, picketed the White House, hunger-struck and was force-fed, lived one wall away from Martha Davis — and they were on opposite sides of the movement's central split, EFS/NAWSA versus Alice Paul's faction. The June 1917 piece, where a Pennsylvania Suffrage Association officer publicly disowns her, is the whole argument in one quote. This is the honest, evidenced suffrage chapter the repo wants, and it needs no invented meetings.

4. "1823, Not 1822" — a short methods piece on the April 1919 Woman's Party meeting notice, showing the address digit at full resolution and explaining why the site will not claim it. Turns the archive's no-fabrication rule into publishable content and pre-empts anyone else who finds the OCR.

5. "Who Actually Lived Here, 1854–1893" — S. Decatur Smith at 1822 Pine in November 1866; John Roset dying at West Philadelphia in August 1870; no Roset ever appearing at a Pine Street address in any Philadelphia daily 1857–1871. Three documents that together reframe the Roset era as ownership-plus-tenancy rather than a family home.

6. "The Block Converts" — 1820 Pine advertised as "Housekeeping Apartments, Finest in Philadelphia" in September 1919 and at $125/month in 1921. Dates the Rittenhouse apartment wave on this exact block two decades before the site's 1941 zoning stamp, and gives GAP 1 a defensible opening.

7. "A Sewing Machine and Eighty Front Doors" — the 1866 Willcox & Gibbs testimonial list as an accidental census of the neighbourhood: Smith at 1822, Professor Booth of the Mint at 1813, Hazlehurst at 1836. A free, quotable, image-friendly substitute for the directory lines the repo says it cannot publish as identifications.

E1. **"756 Pine" is unsearchable in free newspapers, full stop.** Zero hits for "756 Pine" or "756 Pine street" in the entire Penn State PA archive and in all Chronicling America (the three LOC hits are railroad timetables: "leave Auburn at 756 A.M. for Pine grove"). The reason is structural, not a search failure: see E2. Do not run this search again. The renumbering key has to be worked in directories, deeds and tax ledgers, not newspapers.

E2. **The free-newspaper coverage map for Philadelphia, which is the binding constraint on this entire lens.** Complete list of Philadelphia titles with full text on Chronicling America / PSU, with their actual digitized spans:
- The Press — 1857-08-01 to 1865-10-14
- Daily Evening Bulletin — 1864-02-02 to 1870-10-08
- The Evening Telegraph — 1864-07-01 to 1871-06-30
- The American Presbyterian — 1860 to 1869
- Our Daily Fare — 1864 to 1865
- Evening Public Ledger — 1914-09-14 to 1922-12-30
- Gazette of the United States and successors — 1789 to 1801
- La Libera Parola / La Ragione / La Rassegna (Italian-language) — 1917 to 1922
That is it. There is **no free full-text Philadelphia daily for 1802–1856, for 1872–1913, or for 1923 onward.** Consequences:
- 1854–1857, the construction and renumbering window, is unreachable. "756 Pine" could only ever be found in The Press's last four months of 1857 and the 1858 volumes, which are not digitized.
- The whole 1872–1913 span — the $14,000 sale, the 1893 Spencer transfer, the 1899 Duhring/Okie/Ziegler remodel, the 1900 second campaign, the 1901 Rolin-Plumb wedding, the 1904 Davis "at home," the 1905 fireplace permit, the December 1913 tax resistance — has no free newspaper source at all. Those documents exist only behind Newspapers.com, which is why the archive is built on it.
- 1923 to the present is entirely out of reach here.

E3. **The Philadelphia Inquirer and The Philadelphia Times are not in any free full-text archive.** Every one of the archive's 24 unread clippings drawn from those two papers must be retrieved from Newspapers.com by image ID. Two exceptions, both now resolved above: the Evening Telegraph pages of 1870-08-10 (A3) and 1867-04-08 (C2).

E5. Method warnings for whoever picks this up:
- LOC's www.loc.gov Chronicling America search is exact-token against raw OCR; a single garbled character kills the hit. "Spencer kaolin Hockessin" returns zero on a page that contains all three words, because the OCR reads "Hockessln." The PSU Open ONI node's `proxtext=…&proxdistance=N` is far more forgiving and found three Evening Public Ledger pages that LOC's search missed entirely. Run both.
- The legacy `chroniclingamerica.loc.gov/search/pages/results/?format=json` API is dead — it 308s to a www.loc.gov 404. Use `https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/?q=…&fo=json` for LOC, and `https://panewsarchive.psu.edu/search/pages/results/?…&format=json` for PSU.
- Page OCR is at `resource.fulltext_file` in the LOC JSON, and at `/lccn/…/seq-N/ocr.txt` on PSU. Word bounding boxes are at `/lccn/…/seq-N/coordinates/` (gzipped JSON, coordinate space is 4× the JP2) on PSU, and at the LOC `word-coordinates-service` with `format=alto_xml` and no `full_text` flag.
- Crop any address before you believe it: `https://panewsarchive.psu.edu/iiif/<batch-path>.jp2/<x>,<y>,<w>,<h>/1720,/0/default.jpg`, or on LOC `https://tile.loc.gov/image-services/iiif/<service-id>/<x>,<y>,<w>,<h>/full/0/default.jpg` — request size `full`, not an upscale, or you get a blurred low-res tile. Three of my nine strongest OCR leads changed meaning at this step (C1, C2, C3), and two of those three would have been published as facts about this house.
