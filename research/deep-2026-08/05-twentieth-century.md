B3. [GRADE A for the text / GRADE B for the inference] The 1933 Philadelphia Zoning Ordinance explains the "ZONING SEP 11 1941 REGISTERED" stamp on the archive's 1941 deed card — and it explains "five (5) dwelling units" on the 2022 property certificate.
Source: https://www.phila.gov/media/20190516102919/Philadelphia_Zoning_Code_1933.pdf (the ordinance as printed in the Appendix to the Journal of City Council, 1933; 96 pp., image-only — I OCR'd it locally; text saved to scratchpad/z1933.txt)
Verbatim, Appendix p.436 (PDF p.21), ordinance title: "AN ORDINANCE Regulating and restricting the height, location, size, bulk and use of buildings and/or land in Philadelphia, a City of the first class … and providing for the creation of a Bureau of Zoning in the Department of Public Works, which shall be designated as the Bureau of Engineering, Surveys and Zoning and the appointment of a Board of Adjustment…"
  → That is the exact agency that produced every deed-registry card in the archive. The cards are artefacts of this 1933 ordinance.
Verbatim, Appendix p.437 (PDF p.23), definitions: "(d) A single family dwelling shall be a dwelling occupied as the home or residence of one (1) family. (e) A duplex dwelling shall be a dwelling occupied as the home or residence of two (2) families, under one roof… (f) A multiple dwelling shall be a dwelling occupied by three (3) or more families, each living independently of the other."
Verbatim, Appendix p.440 (PDF p.26), "Section 4. NON-CONFORMING USE.": "(2) Any building or the use of any building existing at the time of the passage of this ordinance that does not conform in use, height, location, size or bulk with the regulations of the district in which it is located, shall be considered a non-conforming building or use, and may continue such use in its present location, but shall be subject to the regulations covering non-conforming uses."
Verbatim, Appendix p.503-504 (PDF p.89-90), "PERMITS AND CERTIFICATES": "Use Registration Permit. Use registration permits shall be required for every use commenced after the passage of this ordinance. No buildings shall hereafter be erected, constructed, altered with respect to height and area, added to, or changed in class of use until a use registration permit has been secured, provided, however, that single-family dwellings, duplex dwellings, churches, chapels or other places of worship are exempt from this regulation."
THE INFERENCE (label it clearly as inference, grade B): under this ordinance, a single-family house needed no use registration. A zoning registration stamped at 1822 Pine in September 1941 therefore implies a use OTHER than single-family or duplex — i.e., a multiple dwelling of three or more units. That is the most probable origin of the "Five (5) dwelling units" line still carried on the 2022 property certificate: a legal non-conforming multi-family use registered in the 1933-1941 window and never extinguished. Do not publish this as fact; publish it as "the ordinance that created the stamp, and what the stamp most likely means."

D. CONTEXT SOURCES FOR THE DECLINE / RESTORATION NARRATIVE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

D1. [B] Apartment pressure around the Square began in the 1910s-20s, not after WWII.
Source: https://blog.phillyhistory.org/index.php/2019/12/the-inconvenient-truths-of-rittenhouse-square/ (PhillyHistory Blog, Dec 2019)
Verbatim: the Philadelphia Inquirer in Spring 1924 warned "Like tall chimneys, apartment hotels are circling around Rittenhouse Square," with for-sale signs threatening to "change completely the serenity which long dwelt in that abode of the socially elect." 1924 headlines quoted: "Construction Boom Strikes Rittenhouse Square Section" and "Landmarks are Razed for Fashionable Apartment Structures." Also: "The great houses were shuttered, demolished, or converted to apartments."

D2. [B] Rooming houses were a real Philadelphia phenomenon — but NOT centred on Rittenhouse. Important guardrail.
Source: https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/essays/boarding-and-lodging-houses/ (Encyclopedia of Greater Philadelphia, Wendy Gamber)
Verbatim: "Philadelphia's rooming houses numbered close to a thousand"; the principal lodging-house district lay "just north of Center City, bordered roughly by Race Street, Girard Avenue, Broad, and Sixth Streets"; "By the 1950s, if not earlier, white-collar workers had decamped for apartments and rooming houses had become synonymous with skid row."
Guardrail: the site should say "divided into apartments," which the 1950 census supports for this row, and should NOT say "rooming house" or "boarding house" — nothing supports that for the 1800 block of Pine.

D3. [B] Wartime housing pressure, Philadelphia.
Source: https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/essays/world-war-ii/
Verbatim: "The shipyard grew from a few thousand workers in 1939 to 58,000 workers at its peak." "Philadelphia faced an acute housing shortage because of the influx of people looking for work." "The city of Philadelphia reached its population peak in 1945…"
This is legitimate background for why a 1941 zoning registration and a floor-by-floor 1950 census would look the way they do. It is NOT evidence about this house.

D4. [B] The preservation-movement timeline, with a live contradiction the site should reproduce honestly rather than resolve.
- Philadelphia's preservation ordinance: https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/essays/historic-districts/ — "Philadelphia's historic preservation ordinance was enacted in 1955 under the Home Rule Charter, it did not include the authority to designate historic districts." "When the city's preservation ordinance was overhauled in 1986 it included provisions for creating historic districts."
- District chronology: https://whyy.org/articles/slow-climb-historic-district-status/ (PlanPhilly/WHYY, Jan 6 2010) — Rittenhouse-Fitler designated 1995, "5,828 properties," the largest district; the full list given as Main Street Manayunk (1983), Diamond Street (1986), Park Mall (1990), Rittenhouse-Fitler (1995), Historic Street Paving (1998), Society Hill (1999), Girard Estate (1999), FDR Park (2000), Spring Garden (2001), Old City (2003), Greenbelt Knoll (2006), Tudor East Falls (2009), Parkside (2009). Also: permit applications to the Commission "doubled from approximately 600 annually in the mid-1990s to 1,300+ by 2003."
- The eight-year gap: https://hiddencityphila.org/2025/05/rittenhouse-fitler-historic-district-celebrates-30th-anniversary/ — nominated February 1987, designated February 8, 1995, delayed by "a number of struggles and setbacks owing to some vagaries in the law's language, misinterpretations by property owners, and a lawsuit that worked its way up to the State Supreme Court twice." David Brownlee, then chair of the Commission's Committee on Historic Designation: "When I became chair of the Commission's Committee on Historic Designation, I knew one of the first things I had to do was to bring up the Rittenhouse District."
  Note the contradiction: the designation form itself is dated 9 Feb. 1987 (see A3), which matches Hidden City's February 1987; but the Encyclopedia says district authority only arrived with the 1986 overhaul — consistent, and worth saying plainly: the Rittenhouse-Fitler nomination was filed within months of the city first acquiring the power to designate districts at all, and then took eight years and two trips to the state Supreme Court to survive.
