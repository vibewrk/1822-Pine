⇒ A structural fact worth its own article: **two of John Roset's children — George and Sarah — moved to Chicago and are buried in Graceland Cemetery**, while Ellen and their father stayed in Philadelphia at Woodlands. The Rozets had a Chicago branch. That is the pipeline by which a Philadelphia dry-goods family ends up funding Hull-House.
⇒ CAVEAT: Wikipedia does not mention the Rozets' Philadelphia origins at all, so the John Roset → Sarah Rozet Smith link rests on Find a Grave plus WikiTree. Grade it **B** and say so. And whether Sarah Emily ever actually lived in the Pine Street house remains unproven — she was 15 when the family bought it and married in 1861.

**C9 [A/B] — Drexel: keep the repo's phrasing.**
The Drexel University exhibit gives the marriage as 1850 and adds: "Anthony was devastated by Ellen's death in 1891, shortly before the opening of his legacy project, the Drexel Institute of Art, Science, and Industry." WikiTree specifies 13 Aug 1850 in Philadelphia. Nothing I found places Anthony J. Drexel at this address, and the phila.gov nomination's footnote ("John Roset's daughter married Anthony J. Drexel") is exactly the right altitude. "Documented family connection," never "Drexel lived here."

**C10 [LEAD] — A published book about this family exists.**
The phila.gov nomination cites, for the Rozets' Vienna years: "Grace Mather-Smith and Kelley, Russell P., III, *My Darlings: A Memoir* (Rowman & Littlefield, 2018), 144." Mather-Smith is Mary Rozet Smith's brother's surname line. A 2018 trade-published family memoir touching the Rozets is an obvious next stop and would likely settle several of the above.

NEGATIVE RESULTS — WHERE NOT TO SPEND TIME
═══════════════════════════════════════════

- **Library of Congress / Chronicling America is unreachable from this environment.** The API rate-limited to HTTP 429 across repeated backoffs on both `chroniclingamerica.loc.gov` and `www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america`, and the web UI serves a Cloudflare bot challenge, which I did not attempt to bypass. The searches worth running by hand: phrase "1822 Pine street" and "Martha Davis" restricted to Pennsylvania, 1914–1922 (Evening Public Ledger). This is still the right home for the 1915 ticket notice permalink the repo wants.
- **HathiTrust (babel.hathitrust.org) is behind the same challenge.** Do not plan on it.
- **Internet Archive full-text ("search inside") is not reachable programmatically.** `ia-fts.archive.org` does not resolve; `services/search/beta/page_production/scrape.json` 404s; the SPA at `/search?...&sin=TXT` renders nothing extractable. Metadata search via `advancedsearch.php` works fine. **The workaround that does work, and that I used to make the Howard Spencer find: fetch `https://archive.org/download/<identifier>/<identifier>_djvu.txt` and grep it.** Any digitised directory can be searched this way. Use it.
- **Geni.com** requires JS/login; returns an empty DOM. Not usable here.
- **Find a Grave 403s WebFetch but works fine through the browser tool.** Use the browser; it also exposes memorial-ID hrefs via JS, which is how you walk a family tree quickly.
- **philadelphiabuildings.org 403s WebFetch; works through the browser.** Its tab URLs are not `pj_display_architects.cfm` / `pj_display_references.cfm` (both 404 with a ColdFusion stack trace) — I did not find the correct paths.
- **Penn School Papers finding aid (UNC, https://finding-aids.lib.unc.edu/catalog/03615) names no Davis at all**, and no Lucy Davis among trustees. Lucy Davis's Penn School trusteeship is attested only by the 1914 Woman's Who's Who. Don't expect UNC to corroborate it cheaply.
