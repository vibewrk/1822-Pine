# Deep history research pass — 27 August 2026

Raw returns from a twelve-agent research pass on 1822 Pine Street, its
residents, and the 1800 block. Roughly 1,160 source lookups across census and
directory volumes, free newspaper archives, historic atlases, and institutional
finding aids.

**Nothing in these files is publishable as written.** They are leads with
sources and self-assigned A/B/C grades. Every claim must be re-verified against
its cited source before it appears on the site — that is the site's standing
no-fabrication rule, and this pass exists precisely because several published
claims turned out to be unsourced.

## Independently re-verified in this session

Three findings were confirmed byte-for-byte against Internet Archive OCR, not
just taken from the research return:

- 1857 McElroy's: `Roset John, mer., 756 Pine`
- 1858 McElroy's: `Roset John, nier., 1822 Pine` *(nier. = mer., OCR artifact)*
- 1859 McElroy's: `Broadhead John, 1822 Pine`
- 1871 Gopsill's: `Spencer Howard, pres, 233 S 3d, h 1822 Pine`

Which establishes two things the site had wrong or unknown:

1. **The renumbering is proven.** The same man, in consecutive volumes, with the
   same occupation, moves from 756 Pine to 1822 Pine. That is the key to every
   pre-1858 record about this house.
2. **Howard Spencer is real and lived here in 1871.** He is not a
   mis-transcription of Graham Spencer, and `DEEP-RESEARCH-REPORT.md`'s
   instruction to rename Howard to Graham throughout is wrong. Roset had also
   left the house by 1859 — it was tenanted for most of the period the site
   narrates as "The Roset Years."

## Constraint worth knowing up front

There is no free full-text Philadelphia daily covering 1872–1913. The entire
Spencer remodel, the 1901 wedding, the 1904 "At Home," and the 1913 tax
resistance fall in that gap, which is why the archive is built on paid
newspaper scans and why several claims cannot be verified without a
subscription.
